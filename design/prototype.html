Ink under glass

A conservator's light table: cold optical apparatus over something warm and pigmented. The instrument is glass, the specimen is ink, and the two never blend. Every surface below is built from the token system — the handwriting is a real extraction, not a placeholder.

Try the compositor switch. XFCE may not give us usable backdrop-filter, so glass is built from edges — a gradient fill, an outer hairline, an inner highlight and a cast shadow — with live blur as the optional fifth ingredient. Tinted drops the blur and raises the fill to 0.96. Opaque pre-composites the fill, squares the corners and swaps the shadow for a drawn bevel. It should read as native, never as broken.

The mark is a stroke that has left the page — a baseline, and an upstroke rising off it with a deliberate gap between the two. The gap is the alpha channel, drawn. It also spells the name: ink, lifted. Not a pen: a pen is an input device, and this product is about output.

02

The window

A single-result review-and-retune surface, not a library. The output's half-life is about thirty seconds — it gets made, goes on the clipboard, and lands in someone else's document. What is unavoidable is checking it and fixing it, because the failure modes are invisible in a notification and fatal in a deck.

Press and hold the artwork to see the original underneath — that is how you actually verify an alpha channel. The ground swatches are a viewing control and never merge with the output format in the bottom bar: merging them would make "white" read as destructive and hide that it is a reversible view of the same data.

03

What makes it feel expensive

  • The contact shadow. drop-shadow follows the alpha channel per-pixel, so the shadow traces every stroke's real silhouette including its feathered edge. The ink becomes a sheet of pigment hovering a millimetre above the ground. Two lines of CSS, and it is the most convincing thing in the product.
  • The Void replaces the checkerboard. Photoshop's checker is two greys at 1.35:1 in 8px squares — loud enough to make a soft ink edge unjudgeable, which is exactly wrong for a product whose value is soft edges. The Void is two crossed hairline grids at ±45°, luminance delta under 3%, on a 17px prime pitch so it can never beat against the image's own pixel grid. Recessed, so it reads as a hole in the app.
  • Prussian blue, not system blue. iOS blue is a light-source colour at full saturation. Prussian is a pigment — 1706, the draughtsman's wash for two centuries — 9° greener and 34 points less saturated, so it reads as absorptive rather than emissive. Correct for a substance that sits on something.
  • Warm light, cool dark. The neutrals invert between themes: cotton rag in light, graphite in dark. In light mode you are looking at paper through glass; in dark mode at glass with ink behind it.
  • Engraved separators. One dark hairline with one light hairline beneath it — a rule carved into the glass rather than drawn on it. It is the single detail that stops a popover looking like a web dropdown.
  • The pipeline rail warms on completion. Prussian while the instrument is working, Iron Gall the instant it is ink. The only temperature change in the system, and the whole thesis paying off in 900 ms.
04

Open questions

Three decisions worth arguing with before this gets built:

  • Auto-flip vs suggest. The banner above applies the invert and offers an undo, diverging from the CLI's deliberate suggest-don't-switch. The case for changing: in a CLI a wrong guess makes a wrong file, in a GUI it costs one visible click. The case against: it is still a guess.
  • No library. Recent is capped at 10 items and 7 days with no naming or search, structurally unable to grow into a gallery. Opinionated refusal, not an oversight.
  • True transparency. On a compositing desktop the stage could become a real hole, compositing your ink over your actual wallpaper. Maximally persuasive, and impossible on XFCE without compositing — so it ships off by default, if at all.
